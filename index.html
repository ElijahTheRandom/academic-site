About

I am an incoming Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Texas A&M University, joining Dr. Abdullah Muzahid's group in Fall 2026. I recently completed my B.S. in Computer Science at the University of Alabama in Huntsville, where I was advised by Dr. Chaity Banerjee and Dr. Tathagata Mukherjee, specializing in the internal structures of generative AI and multimodal learning. While my background includes developing autonomous systems and swarm robotics, my current research investigates how high-dimensional representations form inside diffusion models and how we can engineer these systems to be more interpretable and reliable.

My recent work approaches these questions through a digital forensics lens, specifically using diffusion noise priors to localize AI-altered media. My long-term goal is to contribute to the core science of generative modeling across image, video, and sensing domains — current projects range from analyzing diffusion residuals to developing 3D CNN architectures for limited-context video generation.

Dr. Muzahid's work on computer architecture and high-performance computing for LLM training and inference complements this trajectory closely. As frontier generative systems push the limits of memory, throughput, and energy, what a model represents internally and how it runs on hardware become inseparable problems — I am excited to contribute at that intersection.
